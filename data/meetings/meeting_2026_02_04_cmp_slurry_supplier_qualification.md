Meeting: CMP Slurry Supply & Second-Source Qualification — Slurry B-4
Date: 2026-02-04 Time: 08:00 AM CST Location: Zoom (Austin HQ / Penang Fab 2)
Attendees: Nadia Rahman (Supply Chain Manager), Wei Lim (Penang Fab Operations Manager), James Ortiz (Yield Engineer), Lisa Park (PE Manager)
Meeting Type: Supply Chain

Discussion
Lisa Park: On Monday we agreed to move every Volta-7 lot to the low-abrasive Slurry B-4. Nadia, can supply keep up?
Nadia Rahman: Not today. Slurry B-4 is single-sourced from Kestrel Materials in Singapore. Their standard lead time is six weeks, and Penang Fab 2 holds eleven days of stock at the new consumption rate.
Wei Lim: Consumption is higher than planned. The B-4 recipe runs a longer barrier step, so we use about 18% more slurry per wafer than with the old silica slurry.
James Ortiz: Running out isn't an option. If we fall back to the old slurry, Bin 4 bridging comes straight back and we lose ten points of yield.
Nadia Rahman: Kestrel can air-freight two pallets next week at a 22% premium. That covers us to mid-March. In parallel, Halvorsen Chemical offers an equivalent colloidal silica slurry with an eight-week qualification path.
Lisa Park: A second source is mandatory for an automotive part, and NovaDrive's PPAP will ask for it. What does qualification need?
Wei Lim: Three split lots on Platen 1 and Platen 2, inline defect inspection after M3 polish, and a sort yield within one point of B-4.
James Ortiz: I can run the correlation in the yield tracker if I get the inline Klarf data for each split.
Nadia Rahman: Cost-wise, B-4 adds about $1.90 per wafer. Fab 2 runs about 800 wafer starts a month, so that's roughly $18,000 a year, small next to the yield gain.
Lisa Park: Approved. Expedite the air freight and start the Halvorsen qualification now.

Decisions
1. Approved air-freight expedite of two pallets of Slurry B-4 from Kestrel Materials to cover Penang demand through mid-March.
2. Launched second-source qualification of Halvorsen Chemical slurry (three split lots) to remove the single-source risk before the production ramp.
