Meeting: FY2027 Strategy Review — Volta-8 Roadmap & Product Portfolio
Date: 2026-09-15 Time: 09:00 AM CST Location: Executive Boardroom (Austin HQ)
Attendees: Robert Zhang (CEO), Amanda Foster (CTO), Kevin Nash (CFO), Mike Chen (VP Product Engineering), Lisa Park (PE Manager)
Meeting Type: Executive Strategy

Discussion
Robert Zhang: Let's set FY2027. Mike, start with where Volta-7 stands.
Mike Chen: Volta-7 has been in full production since June. Sort yield is holding at 83%, test time at 3.14 seconds, and we shipped 610,000 units to NovaDrive through August with zero field returns.
Kevin Nash: Volta-7 is now 31% of company revenue, up from nothing a year ago. Gross margin is 47%, two points above plan, mostly because test time came in under 3.2 seconds.
Robert Zhang: Good. What did the March crisis cost us?
Kevin Nash: Rev B masks, hot lots, scrapped Rev A inventory and expedite freight came to about $4.6 million. Without the recovery we would have lost the NovaDrive program, which is worth roughly $58 million over its life.
Amanda Foster: The bigger lesson is technical. The clamp substitution and the CMP dishing were both caught late. For Volta-8 I want the automotive library locked in the CAD flow and inline M3 inspection from first silicon, exactly as SOP-PE-042 now requires.
Robert Zhang: What is the Volta-8 scope?
Amanda Foster: A next-generation EV powertrain controller on our 90nm BCD node, with integrated gate drivers for 800-volt inverters and ISO 26262 ASIL-D functional safety. The Portland design center leads, with a tape-out target of Q2 FY2027.
Lisa Park: Product Engineering needs to be in the design reviews from day one. We'll run test program development and reliability socket prep in parallel with mask tooling, as the retrospective agreed.
Kevin Nash: Volta-8 needs about $22 million over 18 months. I can fund it if Volta-7 volume holds and we win a second EV customer.
Mike Chen: Tom Harris has two Tier-1 inverter suppliers evaluating Volta-7 samples now. A second design win de-risks both programs.
Robert Zhang: And the rest of the portfolio?
Amanda Foster: The Eagle-5 MCU family is mature and profitable but flat, so it stays on cost-down only. Falcon-7, our next-generation automotive MCU, is in design in Portland and reaches first silicon in FY2027. The PowerLine PMICs keep growing with the EV market.
Robert Zhang: Then FY2027 has three priorities: protect Volta-7 quality at volume, tape out Volta-8 on time under the new handoff gates, and land a second EV customer.

Decisions
1. Approved the Volta-8 program (90nm BCD, 800V gate drivers, ASIL-D) with a $22M budget and a Q2 FY2027 tape-out target, led by the Portland design center.
2. Set FY2027 company priorities: Volta-7 quality at volume, Volta-8 tape-out under the revised SOP-PE-042 handoff gates, and a second EV customer design win.
